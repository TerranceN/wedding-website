##### ![BlackPin](./Images/PinBlack.webp#inline) **Wedding Location**

The wedding is taking place in the Góral (highlander) region of Poland that Paula’s Mothers family comes from.

###### **Węgierska Górka** <span>(pronounced _“Ven-gier-ska Goor-ka”_, translates to “Hungarian Hill”)</span>

![Węgierska Górka](./Images/PolandTravelWiegierskaGorka.webp#float-left)

A village near Żabnica, where Paula’s mother grew up in. The reception venue is owned by Paula’s uncle and aunt. There are a few local restaurants, spas, hiking trails and WW2 ruins to explore.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### **Żywiec** <span>(pronounced _“Jy-viets”_)</span>

![Żywiec](./Images/PolandTravelZywiec.webp#float-left)

The closest town to Węgierska Górka and the location of the wedding ceremony. The town was first established in the 1300s and the Old Castle, which now hosts a museum of local history and culture, was built in the 1400s. Żywiec is now known for its breweries, but also has a beautiful lake, town square and castle gardens.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Red Pin](./Images/PinRed.webp#inline) **Kraków** <span>(pronounced _“Krak-oof"_)</span>

![Kraków](./Images/PolandTravelKrakow.webp#float-left)

Poland’s cultural and historic capital with buildings dating to the 1200s. Kraków is the closest international airport to the wedding venue and a ~3 hr train ride away. We recommend spending a couple of days in Krakow to experience Poland’s urban culture.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Red Pin](./Images/PinRed.webp#inline) **Zakopane** <span>(pronounced _“Zako-paneh”_)</span>

![Zakopane](./Images/PolandTravelZakopane.webp#float-left)

Located in Poland’s Tatra mountains, Zakopane is known for its beautiful scenery, relaxing spas and Góral highlander culture. Paula and Terrance got engaged while hut-to-hut hiking in the Tatras! See our Hiking Guide for details. Accessible by train from Kraków or car from Węgierska Górka.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Red Pin](./Images/PinRed.webp#inline) **Wrocław** <span>(pronounced _“Vrots-wav”_)</span>

![Wrocław](./Images/PolandTravelWroclaw.webp#float-left)

Where historic and modern Poland meet. Explore the winding stone bridges, colorful markets and lamplit pathways and see what life in Poland really looks like. A great place to stop on the way from Kraków to Prague or Germany.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Blue Pin](./Images/PinBlue.webp#inline) **Gdańsk** <span>(pronounced _“Gd-eye-nsk”_)</span>

![Gdańsk](./Images/PolandTravelGdansk.webp#float-left)

Poland’s main port city and seaside destination. Dock worker strikes here in the 1980s kicked off Poland’s Solidarność (Solidarity) movement which eventually led to the fall of the iron curtain across eastern Europe. Known for its colorful buildings, beautiful main square and seaside.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Blue Pin](./Images/PinBlue.webp#inline) **Warsaw** <span>(“Warszawa” in Polish, pronounced _“Var-Sha-Vah”_)</span>

![Warsaw](./Images/PolandTravelWarsaw.webp#float-left)

Poland’s capital city, 90% of all buildings were leveled during the WW2 Warsaw Uprising, but the old town and castle were rebuilt based on historical paintings and sketches. The rest of the city has a more post-soviet feel, but is an incredible place to visit for museums and WW2 history.

<div class="clear"></div>

###### ![Blue Pin](./Images/PinBlue.webp#inline) **Toruń** <span>(pronounced _“Tor-ooñ”_)</span>

![Toruń](./Images/PolandTravelTorun.webp#float-left)

Toruń is one of the oldest cities in Poland and was first settled in the 8th century. It is known for it’s beautiful old town and Teutonic castle. We have not personally visited Torun, but have heard great things and plan to go someday!
